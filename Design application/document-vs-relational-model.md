Yes — choose based on your application's requirements, specifically: how tree-like vs. interconnected your data is, how much you rely on many-to-many joins, how uniform your record structure is, and whether you typically need whole records or just fragments. But increasingly, the "SQL vs NoSQL" framing itself is a bit outdated — many modern relational databases (Postgres especially) let you get document-model benefits (JSONB columns) while keeping full relational/join power, so the real modern answer is often "use a relational database with good JSON support, and pick JSON vs. normalized tables per field, not per whole application."
